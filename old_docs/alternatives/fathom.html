Audicap vs Fathom: Privacy-First Transcription

Fathom is highly popular for generating free, shareable meeting summaries. But if you value stealth and want to avoid third-party apps recording your desktop, Audicap offers a different approach.

Updated June 28, 20265 minute read

What is the best alternative to Fathom for private notes? If you want to transcribe a Google Meet or Zoom Web call without installing a desktop app or inviting an AI Notetaker bot, Audicap is the ideal alternative. It operates strictly within your Chrome browser, capturing only the tab's audio, ensuring your meeting roster stays clean and your notes remain 100% private.

The tradeoff between automation and privacy

Fathom has grown rapidly because it provides excellent AI meeting summaries for free, integrating deeply into Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams. However, this deep integration requires a tradeoff in privacy.

To use Fathom, you often need to install a desktop application that monitors your calls, grant extensive calendar permissions, or allow an AI bot to visibly join the meeting room. For many users—especially those in healthcare, legal consulting, or freelance journalism—these requirements are dealbreakers.

Audicap: The Browser-Native Approach

Audicap is fundamentally different. It is a lightweight Chrome extension, not a desktop app.

It uses Chrome's native tabCapture API to listen only to the audio playing inside a specific browser tab. It does not monitor your entire computer system, it does not join the meeting as a guest, and it does not automatically upload your recordings to a shared workspace.

When Audicap is a better fit

  • You want zero setup. You just want to click a button and get text, without linking your Google Calendar or Salesforce.
  • You use web apps exclusively. If you use Google Meet or Zoom in the browser, Audicap is the fastest way to get a transcript.
  • You transcribe non-meeting content. Fathom won't transcribe a YouTube tutorial or an online course. Audicap will.
